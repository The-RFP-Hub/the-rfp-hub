# NNNN. Short title, in the imperative

- **Status:** proposed | accepted | superseded by [ADR-NNNN](./NNNN-....md) | deprecated
- **Deciders:** who actually made the call
- **Date:** YYYY-MM-DD
- **Supersedes:** — *(or a link)*

## Context and problem statement

What situation forces a decision, in two or three paragraphs. State the problem, not the
solution. A reader who joins the project a year from now should be able to tell from this
section *why anyone had to decide anything* — if the context is missing, the record is a
changelog entry wearing an ADR's clothes.

Include the constraints that were real at the time (adoption state, headcount, deadlines,
what was already shipped). Constraints expire; recording them is what lets a future reader
tell "this was right then" from "this is right".

## Decision drivers

- The forces that actually weighed on the choice, one per line.
- Keep them falsifiable. "Simplicity" is not a driver; "publishers paste links they have,
  they do not curate arrays of URIs" is.

## Considered options

1. **Option A** — one line saying what it is.
2. **Option B**
3. **Option C**

### Option A

- Good, because …
- Good, because …
- Bad, because …

### Option B

- Good, because …
- Bad, because …

*(Repeat per option. An ADR with one option is not a decision record — it is an announcement.
If there genuinely was no alternative, say so and say why.)*

## Decision outcome

**Chosen: Option X**, because …

State the decision in one sentence a reader can quote, then the reasoning.

## Consequences

- **Good:** what this buys.
- **Bad:** what it costs. Be specific and name who pays. A consequences section with no
  costs in it means the analysis is not finished.
- **Neutral:** what changes without being better or worse.

## Follow-ups

Anything this decision obliges someone to do later: a doc to write, a rule to enforce, a
question deliberately left open. Link issues where they exist.
