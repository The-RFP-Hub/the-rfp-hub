# Marco Barbosa — Builder Interview

**Category:** Builder

Walk me through how you currently find funding opportunities.

It's mostly a combination of ecosystem relationships, X, Telegram, newsletters, and occasionally dedicated grants portals. Over time, people become the most valuable source, rather than websites. By the time an opportunity reaches a public listing, many builders have already heard about it through their network. However, it has happened to me sometimes, even in ecosystems where I've been building for a lot of years, that my application wasn't accepted because I applied within the deadline mentioned in one source, but they had somehow changed the deadline elsewhere. This is extremely frustrating, as the application deadline now matters much more than project quality and relationships and I was actually applying before the deadline mentioned in one source.

---

Where does that process break down?

The biggest problem isn't finding opportunities.. it's understanding whether they're actually worth pursuing.

Every ecosystem has different priorities, evaluation criteria, maturity expectations, reporting requirements, legal constraints, and timelines.

Builders end up spending hours reading documentation only to discover they're not a good fit, the budget is unrealistic, or the program has effectively already identified preferred applicants.

Applying is expensive.

---

What costs you the most time?

Determining genuine alignment. Questions like:

* Is this ecosystem actually interested in products like ours?
* Are they funding infrastructure, consumer apps, AI, DePIN, public goods, or something else?
* How much flexibility exists in the scope?
* Is this a serious funding opportunity or simply an open call?
* Who is making the decision?

That information is rarely explicit.

---

What information is usually missing?

Things like:

* What kinds of projects have actually been funded before?
* Typical grant sizes.
* Number of grants awarded.
* Expected maturity of applicants.
* Whether previous relationships matter.
* Current funding priorities.
* How competitive the process is.
* Average response time.
* Whether the opportunity is still actively reviewed.

Those details matter much more than the application deadline.

---

What would make you use an open RFP hub every week?

Not another directory. I would use it if it became an intelligence layer.

For example:

* Personalized recommendations based on my projects.
* AI explaining why an opportunity is or isn't a good fit.
* Similar projects that previously received funding.
* Probability of success.
* Estimated effort to apply.
* Alerts when funding priorities change.
* Automatic extraction of requirements into structured summaries.

The value isn't aggregation.. it's reducing uncertainty.

---

Which fields matter most?

Besides the basics (deadline, budget, ecosystem), I'd want:

* Funding stage (idea, prototype, scaling).
* Technical focus.
* Geographic restrictions.
* Open-source requirements.
* Token restrictions.
* Equity or IP implications.
* Expected milestones.
* Review timeline.
* Decision makers.
* Links to previously funded projects.
* Current status (open, paused, under review, exhausted).

---

Would you contribute data back?

Yes, if it improves the ecosystem. I'd happily submit corrections, application outcomes, timelines, or feedback after applying.

But contributors should receive something valuable in return, such as better recommendations, reputation, or early notifications.

---

How do you decide whether an opportunity is worth applying to?

The biggest factor is strategic alignment. Funding is useful, but the ecosystem partnership is often more valuable than the grant itself.

I ask:

* Does this ecosystem actually benefit from us succeeding?
* Will they help with adoption?
* Is there a technical or distribution advantage?
* Will this relationship still matter a year from now?

If the answer is no, I usually don't apply.

---

What kills interest?

* Very generic RFPs.
* Unrealistic reporting requirements.
* Tiny grants with large application costs.
* Long delays with no communication.
* Unclear evaluation criteria.
* Opportunities that appear open but are effectively closed.
* Having to rewrite the same information for every application.

---

Website, API, agent, or alerts?

Eventually all three. Personally, I'd prioritize:

1. An AI agent that understands my projects and continuously searches for relevant opportunities.
2. Smart alerts only when there's a strong match.
3. A website for browsing and discovery.
4. An API for integrating opportunities into internal workflows.

I don't want more listings. I want something that understands what I'm building and tells me, "This opportunity is worth your time, and here's why."
