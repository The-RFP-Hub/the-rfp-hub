# My Answers: CoW DAO (publisher)

_Part of: Sov's Answers for RFP_

Hat: CoW DAO Grants Committee member, roughly two years in. Leads all operations and RFPs. Budget context: CIP 63 funded 600K xDAI plus 1.47M COW for grants and 4.99M COW for volume incentives, renewed for 2026 via CIP 82. Council operations are public on the Untitled 

## Core questions
1. How do you currently publish / find / index funding opportunities? Tools, cadence.
Publishing runs through forum.cow.fi on standardized templates: an application template for inbound, an RFP template carrying DAO values for outbound, published grant agreement terms, and a grantee process guide. Awards pass Snapshot. Everything is tracked on a Untitled covering the full lifecycle: Ideation, Scoping, Posted, Sourcing, Awarded, In Progress, Complete. Each grant has a Steward (relationship, milestone checks, payments), a Reviewer (mfw78 for technical, me for BD and partnerships), a Next Action date, and a canonical forum link. Ops sit in a finance tracking spreadsheet, a payment operations SOP, and a mainnet Safe. Cadence: public weekly update every Monday covering applications under review, budget deployment, blockers, and priorities, plus the Community Idea Bounty thread sourcing H2 focus areas (first review Jul 14). Live right now: BYOS RFP with two proposals under committee review, Platform Localization RFP queued for posting, a standing volume based integration request.
2. Where does that process break down? What costs the most time?
Sourcing is the biggest gap. We publish to the forum, so people have to be watching, and the same few teams reply to every RFP. More reach for our proposals would help more than anything else. Secondary friction: assembling the weekly update means pulling inputs scattered across Discord, Slack, Telegram, and sync calls. The pipeline itself is mostly operational. I put it in place because outside the forum we had no good way to track grants through their lifecycle stages, and it powers the regular report outs to the core team.
3. What information is most often missing or wrong in opportunity listings?
We manage our own listings, so nothing is wrong per se, and our volume is relatively low. The gap is that a forum is a poor CRM: fine as the canonical posting surface, weak for tracking applicants, conversations, and state.
4. What would a neutral, open hub need to do for you to use it weekly?
Do the Sourcing stage's job: match a posted RFP to builders who have delivered similar scopes elsewhere, past the CoW forum bubble. Keep status live so the "is this still open" DMs stop. It slots into the existing Monday cadence: the weekly update already reviews the pipeline against forum activity, a hub feed becomes one more input.
5. Which fields actually matter in a listing?
This is our basic grants lifecycle, and our templates are effectively the field list. From the application template: title, team and primary contact, description, grant type (milestone, time based, hybrid), a milestone table (title, due date, funding per milestone), duration with the six month default cap, funding request in xDAI and COW with a cap, payment address, terms acknowledgment. From the RFP template: summary, goal, deliverables with time estimates and dependencies, evaluation criteria, and two deadlines most listings lose, one for community feedback and one for applications. Compliance eligibility also belongs on the listing: our terms exclude US persons and sanctioned jurisdictions, so the schema needs a structured place for that. Pipeline side: stage/status, owner contact (Steward), next action date, canonical forum link.
6. Would you contribute data back? What would make it worth doing?
Conditional yes. Our pipeline, weekly updates, and resources are already public, so anything covered by those is near zero cost. Before committing to more we would need specifics: what the hub requires beyond the publicly verifiable info we already provide today.  I think this will be the default answer from other programs, too.

## Publisher tail
What would make you publish directly to the hub?
Same conditions as questions 4 and 6: if the hub replaces our pipeline tracking, sources proven applicants, and is explicit about what it requires beyond today's public info, direct publishing follows. The forum post stays canonical (our pipeline treats the forum link as the record); the hub ingests or mirrors it as one extra step or an API call. A second CMS to hand maintain is a no.
What would a verified publisher need to include to make the setup step worthwhile?
Proof of budget authority, all of which we already publish: the CIP 63 mandate that makes Grants a sub DAO, the CoW DAO wallet, the grants Safe payouts flow from, the Snapshot space, and the agreement terms pinned on IPFS. Add a named contact and the program docs and verification is done. It should buy distribution, otherwise the setup step is pure cost.
