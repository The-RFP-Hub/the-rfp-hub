# Impeccable — project design context

## Design Context

### Users
Three audiences, one surface. (1) Builders/teams in the Ethereum ecosystem scanning for funding —
arrive anonymous, mid-workday, task-driven ("is there a grant for what I'm building?"); they read
long program descriptions and leave through an external application link. (2) Publishers and
program operators (often devs) maintaining their organization's listings — form-heavy sessions,
correctness over speed. (3) Hub reviewers/admins working queues — repetitive decisions with real
consequences (a click grants publishing rights); they need density, scannability and guardrails.

### Brand Personality
Precise, institutional, impartial. The Hub is neutral public infrastructure: it earns trust the way
a well-run registry does — by being exact, legible and free of salesmanship. Honest copy is already
a house rule (states name their causes; nothing overpromises). The interface should feel like
public infrastructure built by people who care: gov.uk-grade clarity, not startup gloss.

### Aesthetic Direction
Reliable-utilitarian, LIGHT theme only: near-black ink on near-white paper, warm-tinted grays, and
**one rationed accent** — "Oliva cadastral", an olive at a single hue in three steps
(`--accent` `oklch(53% 0.1 112)`, `--accent-ink` `oklch(42% 0.075 112)`, `--accent-soft`
`oklch(95% 0.025 112)`). The accent means one thing and only one thing: **here is where you can
act.** It is spent on textual links (`--accent-ink`, underline always retained), on THE primary
action, the 2px focus ring and the current-navigation underline (`--accent`), and on the hover or
selected tint of a control or row (`--accent-soft`, never alone — always with a border or weight,
since it is 1.12:1 against paper). It is **forbidden** on badges, status, success/error/warning,
pending/rejected, verification verdicts, categories and chart bars: state and hierarchy stay
typographic (weight, size, case, border style), spacing and structure, never carried by color —
which is what keeps the UI colorblind-proof, grayscale-printable and screenshot-legible by
construction rather than by audit. A unit test pins the ration. Density is a feature on work
surfaces (tables, queues); generosity is a feature on reading surfaces (descriptions, forms).
Anti-references: crypto dashboards, dark-glow aesthetics, marketing landing pages, card grids with
icons.

### Design Principles
1. **Ink, not paint.** Emphasis = weight/size/underline/small-caps. If a state matters, say it in
   words; never encode meaning in hue.
2. **Tables are the product.** Rows scan; columns align; numbers right-align; ids are mono; the
   most important cell reads first. No decoration inside cells.
3. **Consequential actions look consequential.** Anything that grants power or publishes to the
   world gets weight, distance from casual clicks, and a confirmation that states the consequence.
4. **The reader's next step is always on screen.** Every state (pending, rejected, empty, error)
   names what happens next and who does it.
5. **One object, one name.** opportunity (in the world) / listing (published here) / submission
   (still pending). "Entry" and "record" never reach the user.
